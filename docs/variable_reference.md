# TCV Dataset Variable Reference

The following table documents all diagnostic and actuator columns in the new TCV parquet dataset. Columns: the physics symbol, a brief physical description, the variable's known relationship to ELMs and confinement modes, and whether the signal can be treated as exogenous (part of a pre-planned control strategy) or whether it is influenced by the evolving plasma discharge.

> **Note on `Halpha_fft`:** The physical meaning of this column is uncertain. The FFT suffix most plausibly indicates a preprocessed or frequency-domain-derived quantity based on the raw H-alpha signal rather than a direct measurement. Confirm with domain scientists before use.

## Shaping

| Column | Symbol | Description | ELM / Mode Relation | Exogenous? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `AREA` | $A_p$ | Cross-sectional area of the plasma boundary, derived from the magnetic equilibrium reconstruction. Increases with higher elongation and triangularity. | Indirectly linked: plasma shape sets the pedestal geometry and peeling-ballooning stability boundary governing ELM onset. Not correlated with individual ELM events. | **No.** Controlled via reference shape targets, but the realized value is measured from the live equilibrium and responds to plasma evolution. |
| `DELTA_BOTTOM` | $\delta_{\text{bottom}}$ | Lower triangularity of the plasma cross-section: measures how strongly the lower boundary deviates from circular toward a D-shape. Typical range 0.2–0.5 on TCV. | Directly affects type-I ELM size and frequency. Reducing triangularity tends to produce smaller ELMs with reduced energy loss per crash. | **No.** The shape control loop tracks a reference; the realized value is measured in real time from the equilibrium and responds to plasma motion. |
| `DELTA_TOP` | $\delta_{\text{top}}$ | Upper triangularity of the plasma cross-section, analogous to $\delta_{\text{bottom}}$ for the upper boundary. TCV has independent control of upper and lower triangularity. | Studies on TCV and AUG show that reducing upper triangularity yields smaller ELMs. Influences pedestal MHD stability. | **No.** Same reasoning as `DELTA_BOTTOM`; measured from the live equilibrium. |
| `GAP_in` | $\Delta_{\text{in}}$ | Radial gap between the inner plasma boundary and the high-field-side wall or limiter. Indicates proximity to inner wall contact. | A slowly varying shape parameter in normal operation. Large ELMs can transiently perturb plasma position, momentarily reducing this gap. | **No.** Derived from the real-time equilibrium; reflects the realized plasma position, not the reference. |
| `GAP_out` | $\Delta_{\text{out}}$ | Radial gap between the outer plasma boundary and the low-field-side wall. Complementary to `GAP_in`. | ELM filaments are ejected outward toward the low-field side; large ELMs may briefly perturb the outer boundary. Otherwise slowly varying. | **No.** Same as `GAP_in`. |
| `KAPPA` | $\kappa$ | Plasma elongation: ratio of the vertical half-height to the horizontal half-width. A circular plasma has $\kappa = 1$; H-mode operation typically requires $\kappa > 1$. | Higher elongation generally favors H-mode access and improves confinement, but does not directly correlate with individual ELM timing. | **No.** Tracked by the shape control loop but measured from the live equilibrium reconstruction. |
| `MAJRAD` | $R_0$ | Major radius of the plasma magnetic axis: radial distance from the torus center to the magnetic axis. Typical value ~0.88 m on TCV. | Not directly related to ELMs. A slowly varying equilibrium parameter. | **Partial.** The target major radius is set by the operator, but the realized value fluctuates with plasma evolution. |
| `MINRAD` | $a$ | Plasma minor radius: horizontal half-width of the plasma cross-section. Together with $R_0$ defines the aspect ratio $R_0/a$. | Determines the pedestal volume and surface area, setting the energy inventory available for ELM release. Not correlated with individual ELM events. | **Partial.** Reference shape is planned, but the realized minor radius is measured from the equilibrium. |
| `R_axis` | $R_{\text{axis}}$ | Radial coordinate of the magnetic axis (the point of maximum poloidal flux). | Slowly varying equilibrium quantity. Large ELMs can cause transient shifts of the magnetic axis. | **No.** Measured from the real-time equilibrium; reflects the instantaneous plasma state. |
| `Z_axis` | $Z_{\text{axis}}$ | Vertical coordinate of the magnetic axis. The position control loop tracks a reference vertical position; deviations can indicate instabilities or control adjustments. | Correlates with plasma dynamics: ELMs produce transient perturbations visible in this signal. One of the standard observables used in TCV confinement studies. | **No.** Measured from the real-time equilibrium; a plasma state variable, not a control input. |
| `VOL` | $V_p$ | Total volume enclosed by the plasma boundary, derived from the equilibrium by integrating the cross-sectional area along the torus. | Sets the global particle and energy inventory. Not correlated with individual ELM events; changes slowly with shape evolution. | **No.** Derived from the live equilibrium; not directly commanded. |

## Emissions

| Column | Symbol | Description | ELM / Mode Relation | Exogenous? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Halpha1` | $\text{PD}_{H\alpha}$ | Intensity of H-alpha (Balmer-alpha, 656 nm) emission at the divertor, measured by a filtered photodiode. Emission arises from excited hydrogen atoms when ELM-ejected particles strike divertor surfaces. Absolute gain varies between shots but is constant within a shot. | Primary ELM indicator: shows sharp, large-amplitude transient peaks during ELM events. Baseline drops at the L-to-H transition and rises at H-to-L; dithering produces rapid low-amplitude fluctuations. | **No.** Direct measurement of plasma boundary emission; entirely determined by the plasma state. |
| `Halpha13` | $\text{PD}_{CIII}$ | Intensity filtered to the CIII (carbon III) emission line near 465 nm. Carbon is a wall impurity in TCV; CIII emission traces the cooler plasma edge and scrape-off layer. | Responds to edge transport events including ELMs, which flush impurities from the pedestal. Provides complementary edge information to `Halpha1` but is a less sharp ELM marker. May show confinement-mode-dependent differences due to changes in edge transport. | **No.** Measurement of plasma edge emission; reflects plasma state. |
| `Halpha_fft` | $\text{PD}_{\text{FFT}}$ | **Uncertain.** The FFT suffix suggests a derived signal rather than a raw photodiode measurement, possibly a running spectral power estimate or bandpass-filtered component of one of the raw H-alpha signals. The exact processing is unconfirmed. | **Unknown without domain confirmation.** If a high-frequency bandpass of `Halpha1`, it would be sensitive to the fast transient structure of ELMs and inter-ELM fluctuations. Confirm with domain scientists before use. | **No.** Derived from plasma emission data; fully determined by the plasma state regardless of processing. |

## Magnetics

| Column | Symbol | Description | ELM / Mode Relation | Exogenous? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `BZERO` | $B_0$ | Vacuum toroidal magnetic field strength at the geometric center of the torus ($R = R_0$), set by the toroidal field coil currents before the shot. On TCV, $|B_0| < 1.54$ T. | Not directly correlated with ELM events within a shot (held constant). Across shots, $B_0$ sets gyrofrequencies, confinement geometry, and the L-H transition power threshold $P_{LH} \propto B_0^{0.82}$. | **Yes.** Set by the operator before the discharge and held constant; does not respond to plasma evolution. |
| `IPLA` | $I_p$ | Realized plasma current, measured in real time by Rogowski coils. Driven by the inductive transformer and actively controlled toward a reference waveform. | Plasma current determines $q_{95}$ and global MHD stability; not a direct ELM trigger. Large ELMs can produce transient perturbations in magnetic measurements. Mode transitions affect the current profile evolution. | **Partial.** The current ramp waveform is pre-planned, but the realized current responds to plasma resistivity and controller feedback; not purely open-loop. |
| `IP` | $I_{p,\textit{ref}}$ | The reference plasma current: the target waveform sent to the plasma current controller, as opposed to the realized current `IPLA`. Represents the operator's intended current trajectory. | Not directly related to ELM events; a smooth, pre-planned waveform that does not respond to plasma dynamics. | **Yes.** The reference waveform is defined before the shot and does not change in response to plasma evolution. |
| `Q95` | $q_{95}$ | Safety factor at the 95% poloidal flux surface: measures how many times a magnetic field line winds toroidally per poloidal revolution near the plasma edge. Determined by plasma current and shape via $q_{95} \propto a^2 B_0 / (R_0 I_p)$. | Directly governs ELM type and onset stability. Type-I ELMs occur preferentially in specific $q_{95}$ windows; lowering $q_{95}$ below 3 can trigger NTM activity and disruptions. TCV research specifically targets $q_{95}$ ranges to control ELM properties. | **No.** A derived equilibrium quantity; responds to changes in $I_p$, shape, and the current profile, all of which evolve during the discharge. |

## Density

| Column | Symbol | Description | ELM / Mode Relation | Exogenous? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `FIR_LIDs_core` | $n_{e,\text{core}}$ | Line-integrated electron density along a central chord through the plasma core, measured by a far-infrared laser interferometer. Phase shift of the laser beam is proportional to the line-integrated $n_e$. | Density rises at the L-to-H transition and falls at H-to-L. ELMs produce brief density drops as particles are expelled. Does not show sharp ELM peaks, making this a smoother observable than `Halpha1`. | **No.** Direct plasma state measurement; influenced by fuelling and transport but not directly commanded. |
| `FIR_LIDs_LFS` | $n_{e,\text{LFS}}$ | Line-integrated electron density along a chord on the low-field side (outer midplane), complementing the core chord. More sensitive to edge and scrape-off layer density. | ELM-driven particle expulsion is most visible on the low-field side, so this signal may show stronger ELM-correlated drops than the core measurement. Mode transitions alter the edge density profile. | **No.** Plasma state measurement; responds to transport and ELM events. |
| `GWfr` | $n_e/n_{\textit{GW}}$ | Greenwald fraction: ratio of the line-averaged electron density to the empirical Greenwald density limit $n_{\text{GW}} = I_p / (\pi a^2)$. The Greenwald limit is an empirical upper bound on sustainable plasma density. | Strongly linked to confinement mode access and ELM behavior. High Greenwald fraction can suppress the L-H transition or alter ELM type. Near $n_e/n_{\text{GW}} \approx 1$ the plasma approaches a density limit and may undergo radiative collapse or disruption. | **No.** The density is influenced by fuelling (partially controlled) and plasma transport (not controlled); the fraction also depends on $I_p$ and $a$, both of which evolve. |
| `Ne_rho_max_grad1` | $\max(n'_{e,\text{edge}})$ | Maximum of the first radial derivative of the electron density profile in the edge region. Characterizes the steepness of the density pedestal. | The density pedestal gradient is a core quantity in peeling-ballooning ELM stability theory. Collapses during each ELM crash and rebuilds in the inter-ELM period. A steeper pedestal accumulates more energy before an ELM. | **No.** Derived from plasma profile measurements; entirely determined by transport and ELM dynamics. |
| `Ne_rho_max_grad2` | $\max(n''_{e,\text{edge}})$ | Maximum of the second radial derivative of the electron density profile in the edge region. Captures the curvature of the density pedestal, providing information about pedestal width and the location of the steepest gradient. | Complements `Ne_rho_max_grad1`. The curvature of the pedestal profile is relevant to gyrokinetic and MHD stability analyses of ELM onset. | **No.** Derived from live profile measurements. |
| `TS_Ne_on_axis` | $n_{e,0}$ | On-axis electron density measured by Thomson scattering, which detects incoherent laser scattering from electrons (signal amplitude $\propto n_e$). Thomson scattering on TCV has lower temporal resolution (order 200 Hz) than interferometry. | Core density is less sensitive to ELMs than edge quantities. Mode transitions produce changes in core density through altered transport. The low sampling rate means individual ELM events may be missed. | **No.** Point measurement of the plasma state. |

## Temperature

| Column | Symbol | Description | ELM / Mode Relation | Exogenous? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SXRcore` | $\textit{SXR}_{\text{core}}$ | Soft X-ray emission intensity from the plasma core, measured by a detector sensitive to photon energies of order 1–10 keV. Emission arises primarily from bremsstrahlung ($\propto n_e^2 Z_{\text{eff}} T_e^{1/2}$) and impurity line radiation. Acts as a proxy for combined core electron temperature and density; not an absolute calibrated measurement of either. | Sensitive to core MHD instabilities (e.g., sawteeth) and changes in core confinement quality. Does not directly capture ELM events, which are edge phenomena, but reflects mode-dependent changes in core transport. | **No.** A plasma emission measurement; fully determined by the plasma state. |
| `Te_rho_max_grad1` | $\max(T'_{e,\text{edge}})$ | Maximum of the first radial derivative of the electron temperature profile in the edge region. Characterizes the steepness of the temperature pedestal, analogous to `Ne_rho_max_grad1` for density. | The temperature pedestal gradient is a primary driver of peeling-ballooning instability leading to type-I ELMs. Collapses during ELM events and rebuilds inter-ELM. The combined pedestal pressure gradient governs the ELM cycle in the standard MHD picture. | **No.** Derived from live profile measurements; governed by plasma dynamics. |
| `Te_rho_max_grad2` | $\max(T''_{e,\text{edge}})$ | Maximum of the second radial derivative of the electron temperature profile in the edge region. Captures pedestal curvature and profile shape, analogous to `Ne_rho_max_grad2`. | Same relevance as `Te_rho_max_grad1`. Provides pedestal shape information for stability analysis. | **No.** Derived from live profile measurements. |
| `TS_Te_on_axis` | $T_{e,0}$ | On-axis electron temperature measured by Thomson scattering. Provides an absolute, calibrated measurement of the core electron temperature in eV or keV. Subject to the same low temporal resolution caveat as `TS_Ne_on_axis`. | Core electron temperature increases in H-mode relative to L-mode due to improved confinement. Not directly sensitive to individual ELM events at the edge. | **No.** Point measurement of the plasma state. |

## Power

| Column | Symbol | Description | ELM / Mode Relation | Exogenous? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `INPWR` | $P_{\textit{in}}$ | Total input power to the plasma: the sum of all heating contributions, typically $P_{\text{in}} = P_{\text{OHM}} + P_{\text{NBI}} + P_{\text{ECRH}}$. Represents the net power driving energy into the plasma. | The excess of $P_{\text{in}}$ above the L-H threshold power $P_{LH}$ determines whether the plasma can sustain H-mode. ELM frequency generally increases with input power in type-I ELMy H-mode. | **No.** A derived sum; includes $P_{\text{OHM}}$, which is not an external actuator (see below). |
| `POHM` | $P_{\textit{OHM}}$ | Ohmic heating power: heat generated by resistive dissipation of the plasma current (Joule heating, $P_{\text{OHM}} \propto \eta j^2$). This is not an external actuator; it is an intrinsic consequence of finite plasma resistivity. The signal is typically sampled at a lower rate and forward-filled, producing a staircase-like temporal structure in the data. | Correlated with plasma current and temperature (higher $T_e$ reduces resistivity and thus $P_{\text{OHM}}$). Its level helps determine proximity to the L-H threshold. Changes slowly and does not directly spike during ELMs. | **No.** Determined by plasma resistivity and current; a plasma state variable, not a commanded input. |
| `PNBI` | $P_{\textit{NBI}}$ | Power of the first neutral beam injection system. NBI heats the plasma by firing high-energy neutral particles that ionize upon entry and transfer kinetic energy via collisions. Operated in step-like plateaus by the operator. | Primary actuator for reaching H-mode on TCV. Sufficient NBI power is required to exceed $P_{LH}$. ELM frequency in type-I H-mode tends to increase with NBI power. Step transitions in NBI are the intended confinement mode control signal. | **Yes.** Open-loop actuator operated on a pre-planned waveform; step transitions do not respond to plasma dynamics on short timescales. |
| `PNBI2` | $P_{\textit{NBI2}}$ | Power of the second neutral beam injection system, physically independent of the first. Separated from `PNBI` in the new dataset. | Same as `PNBI`. Together they provide the total NBI heating power. | **Yes.** Same as `PNBI`. |
| `PECRH` | $P_{\textit{ECRH}}$ | Power of electron cyclotron resonance heating: electromagnetic waves at the electron gyrofrequency are injected and absorbed resonantly. Operated in step-like plateaus. | Provides additional heating to access or maintain H-mode. Effect on ELMs depends on deposition location; on-axis deposition heats core electrons, off-axis deposition can modify edge stability. | **Yes.** Open-loop gyrotron injection; does not respond to real-time plasma state. |
| `P_LH` | $P_{\textit{LH}}$ | L-H transition power threshold: the minimum power crossing the plasma separatrix required to sustain H-mode. Typically a derived quantity computed from empirical multi-machine scaling laws involving $B_0$, $n_e$, and machine dimensions (ITPA scaling: $P_{LH} \propto n_e^{0.78} B_0^{0.82} R_0^{2.03}$). Not a directly measured power. | Defines the confinement mode boundary. When $P_{\text{in}} > P_{LH}$, H-mode access is possible; below it the plasma returns to L-mode. The ratio $P_{\text{in}}/P_{LH}$ is a key operational parameter. | **Partial.** Computed from plasma parameters ($B_0$ is exogenous; $n_e$ is not), so the value evolves with the discharge. If computed from pre-shot targets it is approximately exogenous; if recalculated from live measurements it is not. Confirm provenance. |

## Energy Content

| Column | Symbol | Description | ELM / Mode Relation | Exogenous? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `BETAN` | $\beta_N$ | Normalized plasma beta: $\beta_N = \beta \cdot a B_0 / I_p$ (in %·m·T/MA), where $\beta$ is the ratio of plasma pressure to magnetic pressure. Normalizing by machine parameters allows comparison across devices. | Governs access to neoclassical tearing modes (NTMs), which can be triggered by large ELMs. Elevated in H-mode relative to L-mode. ELMs cause brief drops in $\beta_N$ as stored energy is lost. | **No.** Derived from the plasma equilibrium and pressure profile; entirely determined by the plasma state. |
| `BETAP` | $\beta_p$ | Poloidal beta: ratio of plasma pressure to poloidal magnetic field pressure. Related to the plasma's self-generated bootstrap current, which modifies the current profile and can affect MHD stability. | High $\beta_p$ scenarios are associated with strong bootstrap currents, which can change ELM characteristics. Not a direct ELM trigger, but sets the MHD stability landscape. | **No.** Plasma state variable derived from the equilibrium. |
| `BETAT` | $\beta_t$ | Toroidal beta: ratio of plasma pressure to toroidal magnetic field pressure ($B_0^2 / 2\mu_0$). The most fundamental form of beta; $\beta_N$ is derived from this. | Reflects overall energy content relative to the confining field. Changes at mode transitions; drops during ELM energy loss events. | **No.** Plasma state variable. |
| `Wtot` | $W_{\textit{tot}}$ | Total stored energy in the plasma (thermal plus kinetic), derived from the magnetic equilibrium reconstruction. Measured in joules. | Drops sharply during ELM events proportional to the ELM energy loss $\Delta W_{\text{ELM}}$. Increases in H-mode relative to L-mode due to the edge transport barrier. Rebuilds inter-ELM as heating restores the pedestal. | **No.** A direct measure of the plasma energy state. |
| `DML` | $\text{DML}$ | Diamagnetic loop signal: measures the toroidal diamagnetic flux generated by the confined plasma, correlated with the total stored energy $W_{\text{tot}}$. Exhibits quasi-harmonic structure at high frequencies, partly attributed to anti-aliasing in the data processing pipeline. | Responds to ELMs: each crash produces a dip corresponding to $\Delta W_{\text{ELM}}$. Mode transitions produce step changes. Smoother than `Halpha1` but contains dynamically rich structure. | **No.** A plasma state measurement. |
| `H98y2calc` | $H_{\textit{98y2}}$ | ITER H98(y,2) confinement enhancement factor: ratio of the measured energy confinement time $\tau_E$ to the IPB98(y,2) empirical multi-machine scaling law prediction. $H_{98} = 1$ corresponds to standard H-mode performance; $H_{98} > 1$ indicates enhanced confinement. | Increases when the plasma enters H-mode (typically $H_{98} \geq 0.9$–$1.0$) and decreases in L-mode. ELMs reduce $\tau_E$ transiently. A key figure of merit for reactor relevance. | **No.** Derived from measured $\tau_E$ and plasma parameters; a plasma state diagnostic. |

## Radiation

| Column | Symbol | Description | ELM / Mode Relation | Exogenous? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Prad` | $P_{\textit{rad}}$ | Total radiated power from the plasma, integrated over the full plasma volume from a bolometer array. Radiation is a significant energy loss channel; the net power crossing the separatrix is $P_{\text{sep}} = P_{\text{in}} - P_{\text{rad}} - dW/dt$. | High radiation reduces $P_{\text{sep}}$ and can prevent H-mode access. Sudden rises in total radiation may precede disruptions. ELMs produce brief spikes of radiated power as ejected hot plasma recombines on wall surfaces. | **No.** Determined by plasma temperature, density, and impurity content; a plasma state variable. |
| `PradBulk` | $P_{\textit{rad},\text{bulk}}$ | Radiated power from the bulk plasma volume, excluding contributions from the divertor and scrape-off layer. Separating bulk from divertor radiation helps distinguish core impurity accumulation from edge recycling effects. | Elevated bulk radiation indicates impurity accumulation in the core, which degrades confinement. ELMs help flush impurities from the pedestal, reducing bulk radiation transiently. In H-mode, impurity accumulation can reduce $H_{98}$ and destabilize the plasma. | **No.** Plasma state variable. |
| `PSOL_RT` | $P_{\textit{rad},\text{SOL}}$ | Radiated power in the scrape-off layer (SOL): the region outside the last closed flux surface where field lines connect to the divertor. May be computed in real time from bolometer signals. | ELMs deposit intense heat pulses into the SOL, transiently raising $P_{\text{rad},\text{SOL}}$. In detached divertor operation, high SOL radiation is intentional. The ratio of SOL radiation to total input power is a key divertor heat exhaust metric. | **No.** Plasma state variable determined by transport and ELM dynamics. |

## Other

| Column | Symbol | Description | ELM / Mode Relation | Exogenous? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `LI` | $l_i$ | Internal inductance: a dimensionless measure of the peaking of the toroidal current density profile, defined as the volume-averaged poloidal magnetic field energy normalized to the poloidal field at the plasma boundary. A flat current profile gives lower $l_i$; a peaked profile gives higher $l_i$. | The current profile shape affects MHD stability and ELM characteristics. Changes in $l_i$ during a shot reflect current profile evolution (resistive diffusion, bootstrap current). Not directly correlated with individual ELM events. | **No.** Determined by the evolving current profile; not directly commanded. |
| `ZEFF` | $Z_{\textit{eff}}$ | Effective ion charge: the charge-weighted average $Z_{\text{eff}} = \sum_j n_j Z_j^2 / n_e$ over all ion species including impurities. A pure deuterium plasma has $Z_{\text{eff}} = 1$; impurity influx raises this value. Typically inferred from bremsstrahlung measurements. | Impurity content affects resistivity, radiated power, and confinement quality. ELMs flush impurities from the pedestal, producing transient drops in edge $Z_{\text{eff}}$. Elevated $Z_{\text{eff}}$ reduces thermal conductivity and can suppress H-mode access. | **No.** Determined by wall recycling, impurity influx, and plasma transport; a plasma state variable. |
| `nu_e_star` | $\nu_{e,\text{ped}}^{*}$ | Electron collisionality at the pedestal top: a dimensionless parameter measuring the ratio of the electron collision frequency to the electron bounce frequency in the pedestal region. Low collisionality ($\nu^* \ll 1$) is the ITER-relevant regime. | Pedestal collisionality governs ELM suppression by resonant magnetic perturbations (RMPs): suppression in DIII-D and AUG is observed preferentially at low $\nu^*_{\text{ped}}$. Type-I ELM crash size correlates with $\nu^*_{\text{ped}}$. Access to ELM-free regimes often requires sufficiently low collisionality. | **No.** Derived from pedestal $T_e$ and $n_e$ measurements; entirely determined by the plasma state. |
| `Vloop` | $V_{\textit{loop}}$ | Loop voltage: the toroidal electric field driving the inductive plasma current, measured as the voltage induced around the torus. Approaches zero in fully non-inductive scenarios. Related to plasma resistivity via $V_{\text{loop}} \approx \eta j \cdot 2\pi R_0$. | Decreases as plasma temperature rises (higher $T_e$ reduces resistivity). Mode transitions produce changes in $V_{\text{loop}}$ due to altered temperature and bootstrap current fraction. Not directly correlated with individual ELM events. | **Partial.** The transformer waveform is pre-planned, but the realized loop voltage depends on plasma resistivity and bootstrap current, both of which evolve with the discharge. |
